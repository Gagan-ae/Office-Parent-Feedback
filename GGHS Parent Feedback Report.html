How each area was rated

Areas are sorted from weakest to strongest. Each bar shows how many of the 118 parents gave each score.

1234 (highest)
  • International collaborations is the weakest area (3.12). 25 parents (21%) rated it 1 or 2, and only 49 gave it a 4. Ten parents gave it the lowest score.
  • Learning apps (Accadly, Go Sharpner, SHIN) come second (3.27). 18 parents rated them 1 or 2. Academics is the lowest-scoring group overall (3.40) because of these two areas; curriculum delivery (3.64) and books (3.58) score well.
  • Uniform arrangements (3.36) drew 8 ratings of 1, the second-highest count of bottom scores for any area.
  • Sports (3.42), ERP / Parent App (3.46), transport (3.47) and communication (3.48) form a second band, each with 11 to 14 parents rating 1 or 2.
  • Strongest areas: cleanliness (3.68), infrastructure (3.65), curriculum, security and staff support (3.64 each).
  • Communication tracks the overall rating most closely. Of all 17 specific areas, a parent's communication score is the one most strongly related to their "overall school environment" score (correlation 0.61); infrastructure, cleanliness and security are the least related (0.34).

What the data says about the parents

  • Most parents are content. 63 parents (53%) gave no rating below 3, and 21 gave 4 in every area.
  • Dissatisfaction is concentrated. 12 parents (10%) gave five or more ratings of 1 or 2, and together they account for 86 of the 163 low ratings (53%). Resolving their concerns individually would remove half of the low scores.
  • The largest group is lukewarm. 39 parents gave only 3s and 4s. They are not unhappy, but 30% of all ratings were a 3, so there is room to move them to 4.
  • Child safety needs individual attention. 4 parents rated security and child safety 1 or 2. The number is small, but each case should be heard personally. Filter the list below by "Security & child safety".
  • Three parents marked 3 for every question (Kalpak Dasgupta, Niresh Mehta, Ashwani Kumar Singh). This may be a quick fill rather than a considered view, so a short call will tell more than the form.

Limits of this data: the form has no comment field in use (the last column is empty) and no class or section, so it shows where parents are unhappy but not why, and it cannot be split by grade. All responses came in on one day between 08:35 and 14:03. Six phone numbers submitted twice; five of those pairs have different ratings (possibly two children), so all 118 responses are kept and the repeats are marked in the list. The scale is read as 4 = best, which fits the response pattern.

Measures to take

Because the form does not record reasons, the first step in every area is to ask. The actions below are ordered by how many parents each one affects.

1. Call the 21 Priority 1 parents within a week

Owner: Principal / coordinators · Affects the 21 parents averaging 3.0 or below
  • A senior staff member calls each parent, starting with those who gave the most ratings of 1 or 2.
  • Record the specific complaint, the child's class, and what was promised, in one shared sheet.
  • Call back within 15 days to confirm the issue is closed.

2. International collaborations

Owner: Academic head · 69 parents rated 3 or below
  • Check how many students actually took part this year. A low score here often means most children were never included.
  • Publish a calendar of exchanges and virtual sessions, with a rotation so every class takes part at least once a year.
  • Share photos and student work from each session with all parents, not only the participants'.

3. Learning apps

Owner: IT / academic coordinators · 62 parents rated 3 or below
  • Ask low-rating parents which app is the problem: login trouble, too much screen time, or unclear benefit.
  • Run a short parent orientation, and send a one-page guide for each app.
  • Send a monthly usage and progress summary per child so parents see the value.
  • Review any app with low usage before renewing it.

4. Uniform arrangements

Owner: Admin office · 52 parents rated 3 or below, 8 gave the lowest score
  • Find out whether the issue is price, quality, stock and sizes, or the vendor's service.
  • Agree stock levels and exchange terms with the vendor before the new session.
  • Hold a uniform counter at school on fixed dates and announce it early.

5. Communication, responsiveness and the Parent App

Owner: Front office / MIS · About 50 parents rated each of these 3 or below
  • Set a response standard, for example every parent query acknowledged within one working day, and track it.
  • Name one contact person per class for parent queries.
  • Collect the specific ERP / Parent App faults from low-rating parents and raise them with the vendor.
  • Use one official channel for circulars so parents do not miss notices. This area has the strongest link to how parents rate the school overall.

6. Sports and transport

Owner: Sports head, transport in-charge · 53 and 46 parents rated 3 or below
  • Sports: share the weekly games timetable, and check that every child, not only team players, gets regular play and coaching time.
  • Transport: ask the 14 low-rating parents for their route and the issue (timing, crowding, staff behaviour, tracking), then fix it route by route.

7. Security and child safety cases

Owner: Principal · 4 parents rated 1 or 2
  • Meet each of these parents in person and document the concern and the action taken.
  • Treat any specific incident raised under the school's child protection procedure.

8. Improve the next survey

Owner: MIS
  • Add student name, class and section, and a "what should we improve?" comment box.
  • Ask for a reason whenever a parent chooses 1 or 2.
  • Allow one response per child, and repeat the survey each term to measure change against these scores.
